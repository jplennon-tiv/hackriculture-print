Fast-growing radishes provide mild, juicy spring roots and suit spare corners, window boxes, protected sowings
or gaps between slower crops. Pick small red or red-white types young: heat, age and drought make them hot,
woody, hollow or soft. Longer-growing winter radishes come in white, pink, black or red-white. Grate, pickle,
braise or stir-fry them; autumn-lifted roots also store in cool, damp conditions.

Recommended varieties
SPRING/SUMMER
French Breakfast 3
A familiar long red-and-white
summer radish. Sow small batches;
pick promptly for crisp, mildly
peppery roots.
SPRING/SUMMER
Cherry Belle
A round, bright-red salad radish for
quick crops in beds or containers.
SPRING/SUMMER
Scarlet Globe
A round red summer radish for
quick, repeat crops. Harvest young
before roots become coarse.
WINTER
China Rose
A pink, long-rooted winter radish for autumn use,
contrasting with round black storage types.
WINTER
Black Spanish Round
A black-skinned winter radish with firm white flesh,
grown for large autumn roots, not quick summer crops.
Key risks
Checked growth /
poor root quality
Turnips, swedes and
radishes need steady, quick
growth.
Flea beetle
Tiny beetles pepper
seedling leaves with holes
and can check young crops.
Bolting
Winter radishes sown
before July may flower
before forming usable roots.
Cabbage root fly
Late and winter radishes are
often tunnelled by cabbage
root fly maggots, especially
after May sowings.
