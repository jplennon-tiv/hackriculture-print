Beetroot’s sweet, earthy roots suit raw salads, hot dishes, pickles, borsch and chutneys; young leaves are edible
too. This cool-climate crop needs well-drained, humus-rich soil and steady moisture. Growth starts slowly, then
roots swell quickly. Sow small batches for summer, autumn and storage crops. Lift before roots become woody
or leathery. In mild winters some can stay outside, but severe frost can freeze and rot them.

Key risks
Bolting
Plants sometimes run to
seed before roots have
developed.
Fanging
Forked roots are usually
caused by adding fresh
manure shortly before
sowing.
Mangold fly (leaf
miner)
Small white grubs burrow
inside the leaves, causing
tunnels which later turn into
blisters.
Heart rot
Leaves wilt in summer and
the tops of the roots develop
brown, sunken patches.
