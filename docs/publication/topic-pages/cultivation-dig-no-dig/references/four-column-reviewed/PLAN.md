## Titles and scope

Contents heading: **Growing Techniques**.

First section: **Cultivation: Dig or No-Dig** (agreed wording, interpreting
“ig” in the brief as “Dig”). List the section once under Growing Techniques;
the three part headings appear on their own pages.

Use “digging” rather than “conventional growing” to identify the cultivation
method clearly. Both routes use the project's organic approach. Help readers
choose and start, with equal space and fair treatment of each method. Compare
the same conditions and reader questions; do not manufacture equal benefits
where evidence differs or select an overall winner.

## Matrix and limits

Use four equally weighted columns, as requested after John's first review:
**Dig pro, Dig con, No-dig pro, No-dig con**. Keep related advantages and limitations
on the same row, without scores or an overall winner. The six comparison topics
are retained as editable row metadata rather than a fifth printed column:

- Initial work and physical effort.
- Ongoing maintenance and weed management.
- Compost or other organic-matter requirements.
- Tools, materials and cost.
- Starting soil/plot condition and drainage considerations.
- Practical limitations and flexibility to change methods.

Distinguish starting a plot from maintaining an established bed. Cover one-off
preparation/transitions briefly where supported, without presenting the methods
as permanent opposing camps. Raised-bed construction, compost making, drainage
engineering, detailed soil biology, trial histories and advanced digging variants
are outside this overview. Retain essential conditions and safety; leave depth
and specialist detail to other books.
