# Tomato, Greenhouse: dates, growth habit and wording

10 November 2026. [Review both editions](REVIEW.html). **Approved by John in both editions.**

January is retained as a pale, exceptional sowing month. The canonical advice
and Garden Organic both support a heated-greenhouse start; Quick facts now says
“Late Feb–Mar. Late Jan only with a heated greenhouse and bright light.” Bright
February–March remains the normal calendar band. [Sources and reasoning](../../../../../../hackriculture-resources/summaries/tomato-greenhouse-review/SOURCES.md).

All eight Quick facts are regrouped. The longer sowing, container and bed-spacing
entries share one column; shorter facts and Ready in share the other. Original
values, icons and type sizes remain, apart from the clearer January condition.
Fact-column heights are 30.46 / 31.92 mm in both editions.

The fuller introduction explains training a cordon’s single main stem and
leaving bush shoots and growing tips intact. The reminder now gives the bush
action directly. The vague care qualification becomes a seed-packet instruction
for semi-determinate varieties. All five original varieties remain; Sungold F1, Alicante,
Shirley and Sweet Million F1 already explicitly identify their cordon habit.
The original summer/autumn introduction phrase is not repeated because the
adjacent season label gives it.

Red Alert is added as the sixth recommended variety, clearly labelled **Bush**.
It already exists in the canonical catalogue; RHS and Thompson & Morgan confirm
greenhouse/container suitability. Sweet Million and San Marzano descriptions are
slightly shortened to preserve their meaning at the narrower three-column width.

Both Final tips now use John’s supplied wording verbatim, with the approved
Style-A pruning and mulching icons replacing protection and feeding. Core care
entry 1 retains the gradual-removal and healthy-foliage cautions; the new mulching
entry is 10. Their short text and the Final tips extract store the two requested
sentences and icons. [Guarded save receipt](SAVE-RECEIPT.json) records the exact
prior bytes, scoped fields and validation. The Final tips extract is now a draft
with current dependencies and checksum. Other historical extract/layout approvals
remain untouched and stale where affected; no manual locks were changed.

John’s exact corrections are applied: “soil with added compost” and “apple or
banana can help ripen the remaining tomatoes”. No routine pH/liming text occurs
in the printed guide, so that standard check requires no change.

John’s latest correction removes “keep healthy leaves to feed and shade fruit”
from Looking after the crop, joining the remaining sentences with a full stop.
This book-only deletion leaves both Final tips unchanged; the earlier core-data
transaction is not repeated. Both opening pages and all other page text are exact.

Both final PDFs remain two 185 × 240 mm pages, folios 92–93, with all nine
selected pest rows, original artwork and four See also links. The original
Magnesium deficiency omission remains; no further rows were cut. Footer gaps
are 6.31 / 6.54 mm in both editions. The initial longer draft failed the opening
clearance; a tighter introduction and fact widths resolved it at unchanged type
sizes. The final spreads were inspected against the original.

The requested care and Final tips source fields are updated; the canonical
variety catalogue, all other source advice, original review PDFs, full readers
and the main book remain unchanged. Sweet Corn’s approval is saved separately. Checks are
confined to this revision, its source hashes, PDF text and reader links; no
application tests or shared-code changes were needed. All eight facts and every
changed passage were checked in the actual PDF text; folios, trim size and all
four PDF destinations are correct. The reader works online and offline. The source hash matches the guarded save receipt, the backup is exact, and
baseline hashes and the approved Sweet Corn files are unchanged.
