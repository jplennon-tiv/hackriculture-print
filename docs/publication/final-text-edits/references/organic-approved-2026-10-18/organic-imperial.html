Growing Techniques - Organic growing

Organic growing The core ideas

Organic growing begins with a useful bit of housekeeping: keep as much plant material as possible in the garden, turn it into compost and return it to the soil.

Look after the ground

Use mature compost to improve the soil and replenish what crops take from it. Organic soil needs to breathe, so avoid compaction: keep feet and barrow wheels on the paths. Respect the life under your feet: don't light fires on the soil, and don't poison the ground with chemicals that you wouldn't be prepared to eat yourself.

Work with the place you have

A plant in the right soil, with correct levels of sunshine and water, will almost always succeed. Grow the crops and varieties that suit your ground - you will find this out with time and experience; every site and every plot is different. Keep a record of successes as well as failures; work with Nature to grow what is right for your garden and your garden will ultimately flourish.

Deal with the cause

Look closely before reaching for a remedy. Check moisture, roots and recent weather. Hand-pick damaging pests where practical and fit suitable crop protection: know the likely arrival times of pests in your area and net vulnerable plants before the pests arrive.

Experiment on a small patch

A thick hay-and-straw mulch that works well in a drier climate can shelter slugs on cold, wet ground. Try new ideas on a manageable area before changing the whole garden. Keep what works, understand why it works, and feel free to adapt it.
