Source order

The pea and broad-bean records supply the root-retention method; the pea record also explains humus and moisture. John’s manuscript and Why We Make Compost video supply the practical theme of returning plant material to grow later crops. The video captions are machine transcripts, so they have not been used as exact scientific definitions. Online sources fill the mechanism and terminology gaps.

Online explanations

  • AHDB — Biological fixation of nitrogen by rhizobia — Root-nodule bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen; residues release nitrogen through mineralisation; contribution varies.
  • RHS — Organic matter: how to use in the garden — Photosynthesis, plant residues, decomposition and carbon dioxide; organic matter binds soil particles, retains nutrients and moisture; some forms last longer.
  • Garden Organic — The function of plant nutrients — Pages 2–4: organic and mineral phosphorus pools, availability and root function; potassium pools, water balance, fruit development, compost/comfrey recycling; other nutrients and uptake conditions.

What the diagrams mean

The carbon loop traces carbon from air to plants and decaying remains, then back to air. The humus note explains the portion retained in soil organic matter. The nitrogen strip shows the useful legume-to-following-crop pathway; release happens during decay, and is a contribution rather than a guaranteed complete feed.
