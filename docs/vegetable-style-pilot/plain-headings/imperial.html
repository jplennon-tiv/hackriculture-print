Carrots.

Growing guide

A practical guide to growing carrots.

DIFFICULTYNot difficult2/5
Summer / autumn crops

Freshly pulled carrots have a sweet, earthy flavour that shop-bought roots rarely match. They are also one of the more awkward crops to establish well: germination can be slow, seedlings are vulnerable to slugs, and the roots need deep, fine, stone-free soil if they are to grow straight and tender. Once growing, however, carrots are very rewarding, especially if sown in short successions so that roots can be lifted from early summer into autumn. Good moisture, careful thinning, and protection from carrot fly make a big difference.

Key risks.

CARROT FLY

Tell-tale signs on carrots are reddish leaves which wilt in sunny weather and later turn yellow.

FANGING

Fanging is usually caused by adding manure or compost to the soil shortly before seed sowing.

SMALL ROOTS

Aphids and virus will stunt growth and reduce yield, but even in the absence of pests and diseases many gardeners produce disappointingly small carrots.

SPLITTING

Much more serious than fanging of carrots, because the roots will not store.

Growing & harvesting

Prepare the soil well.

Deep, fertile, stone-free and sandy soil is ideal.

Avoid fresh manure.

Follow a crop manured the previous year.

Weed young seedlings.

Weeds can easily smother them.

Soil & preparation.

  1. Use deep, fertile, sandy, stone-free soil; choose short-rooted varieties for heavy or stony ground. Improve heavy clay well before sowing.
  2. Follow a previously manured crop. Avoid fresh manure, which encourages forking; lime acid soil in winter and prepare a fine seedbed.

Sowing & thinning

1 Sow thinly

Sow direct, very thinly, in shallow drills.

2 Cover lightly

Cover lightly with fine soil or compost.

3 Thin later

Thin young seedlings to the final spacing below.

Seed depth 1/2 in.Rows 6 in. between rowsFinal plants Thin to 2-3 in. apart

Mix seed with sand, sow into warmed soil and keep the seedbed moist.

Looking after your crop.

  1. Thin only where necessary, in the evening or damp weather; water dry ground first. Firm soil afterwards and remove thinnings: bruised roots attract carrot fly.
  2. Weed carefully while seedlings are small. Avoid hoeing close to established carrots or loosening soil around their roots.
  3. Water deeply and evenly during dry spells. Sudden heavy watering after drought may split roots.
  4. Keep young beds bare to reduce slug shelter; clear early weeds before later sowings.

Harvesting & storage

  1. Lift small carrots as needed from June; baby roots may be ready in about eight weeks. Work from one row end and ease roots with a fork to avoid disturbance.
  2. Usually lift maincrops for storage in October. Lift sooner if carrot fly appears, and before hard frost or very wet weather causes splitting.
  3. Store only sound roots. Trim leaves to about 1/2 in. above the crown; keep roots separate in sand, cool and ventilated just above freezing. Inspect for rot; sound roots can keep until March.
