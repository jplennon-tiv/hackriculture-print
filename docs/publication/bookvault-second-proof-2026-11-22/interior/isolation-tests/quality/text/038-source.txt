Celeriac, or turnip-rooted celery, is grown for its swollen stem-base. Its earthy celery flavour suits soups, stews,
salads and winter dishes; lifted roots keep into spring if stored cool. It needs less blanching and earthing-up
work than celery, but still needs a full season, a good start under cover and rich, moisture-retentive soil.
Shallow, fibrous roots suffer in dry ground: water steadily in dry weather. Heavy, well-composted soil suits it
well; varieties are broadly similar.

Recommended varieties
Prinz
A dependable celeriac for rounded roots and useful
kitchen quality. A strong first choice for the main crop.
Monarch
A modern celeriac with pale flesh and comparatively
smooth roots. A useful addition for easier preparation
in the kitchen.
Asterix F1
An early hybrid producing large, round roots for
autumn harvesting and storage. Useful bolting
resistance helps support a dependable crop.
Giant Prague
A long-established celeriac with large roots. Retain as
a heritage alternative where seed cost and traditional
varieties matter.

Celeriac is hardy, but
