Jerusalem artichokes are not true artichokes at all, but vigorous sunflower relatives grown for their knobbly
underground tubers. They are hardy, reliable and useful for winter soups, stews and grated salads, with a sweet,
nutty flavour which is not to everybody's taste and may not suit every digestion. The plants grow very tall in rich
soil, making them useful as a screen or windbreak but also capable of robbing light from lower crops if planted
in a long row. They are easy to grow from tubers, but hard to eradicate once established because even small
pieces left in the soil can regrow.

Recommended varieties
Fuseau
Smooth, elongated tubers make this a practical kitchen
choice, with less fiddly preparation than very knobbly
selections.
Dwarf Sunray
A shorter-growing option with pale, relatively smooth
tubers. Worth considering where tall Jerusalem
artichokes would be awkward.
Small Wonder
A compact Jerusalem artichoke sold for containers and
small plots, with tubers clustered near the stem.
Gerard
A French variety with red skin and fine flavour. The
tubers are rounder than Fuseau; check availability with
specialist suppliers.
Key risks
Sclerotinia rot
Stem bases rot and may carry fluffy
white mould with black, seed-like
resting bodies.
Slugs
Slugs may nibble spring shoots;
established Jerusalem artichokes
usually outgrow light leaf damage.
Mice
Mice may burrow among tubers in
winter and eat stored or unlifted roots.
