Carrot Current book pages 36–37

The direct-sowing method is sound. The order can be clearer, sand has become an instruction rather than an option, and the thinning instruction is split between this section and later care. No planting-out date is needed.

Before 50 words · current planting block

1 Sow thinly. Sow direct, very thinly, in shallow drills.

2 Cover lightly. Cover lightly with fine soil or compost.

3 Thin later. Thin seedlings to the final spacing in Quick facts.

Mix seed with sand, sow into warmed soil and keep the seedbed moist. For November–December roots, sow a short-rooted variety in August; cover with cloches from October.

After — proposed 125 words · +75

Sow direct. Sow where the carrots will grow, usually April–June. Wait if the soil is cold or wet. Make shallow drills ½ in. deep and 6 in. apart, watering them first if dry. Sow very thinly; mixing the seed with dry sand can help. Cover with fine soil or compost, firm gently and keep moist while the seeds germinate.

Thin only where needed. Leave 2–3 in. between plants. Water dry soil first, then thin in damp weather or in the evening. Firm the soil afterwards and bury or destroy the thinnings: bruised carrots attract carrot fly.

Extend the season. For an early crop, sow a short-rooted variety under cloches in early March, or in a sheltered spot in late March or April. For November–December roots, sow in August and cover with cloches from October.

Beetroot Current book pages 26–27

Direct sowing and module clumps are both present, but there is no complete clump-planting instruction. The 4 in. spacing is for single plants. Seed clusters, retained seedlings and whole planted clumps need distinct wording.

Before 49 words · current planting block

1 Sow seed clusters. Sow clusters thinly; cover and keep moist.

2 Thin seedlings. Thin direct sowings early to single plants.

Raise early crops in modules; plant clumps intact. Quick facts spacing is for individual, direct-sown plants.

Soak clusters for hours or overnight. Sow warm for early-spring planting; continue into early summer for roots before winter.

After — proposed 154 words · +105

Sow direct. Sow April–June, or from late March when conditions suit. Sow two seed clusters at each position, 1 in. deep and 4 in. apart, in rows 12 in. apart. Keep the seedbed moist. Soaking the clusters for several hours or overnight can help germination.

Thin to single plants. A corky seed cluster can produce several seedlings. When they are about 1 in. high, thin to one plant at each position.

Alternative: raise early clumps. Sow a bolt-resistant variety under cover with warmth in February–March: three or four seed clusters per module, 1 in. deep, thinning to about four or five seedlings. Harden them off and plant the clumps intact in suitable spring weather, once well rooted and at least 2 in. high. Allow 12 in. between clumps in each direction. Twist out the largest roots first, leaving the others to grow.

For later roots. Sow in late May or June for storage. An early July sowing can still give medium-sized roots before winter in favourable conditions.

Leek Current book pages 62–63

The current block starts with the final transplant operation, then returns to sowing. The pencil-thick individual-plant method and the young intact-clump method need separate sequences, spacings and planting instructions.

Before 60 words · current planting block

1 Lower the leek. Plant May–July: one pencil-thick leek per hole.

2 Water into the hole. Water in rather than filling the hole with dry soil.

Transplant hole. Dibbed, 6 in. deep

Sow thinly in a seedbed or modules.

Use fresh seed. Sow outdoors in late March–April; start very early or exhibition crops indoors in late winter.

Clumps: sow 3–4 per module; plant small. Use strong plants; don’t trim healthy roots/leaves.

After — proposed 182 words · +122

Sow in a nursery bed. Sow fresh seed thinly, ⅝ in. deep, in late March or April. This is a small bed for raising plants to move later. Waiting for suitable spring conditions reduces the risk of autumn bolting.

Lift and transplant. Usually May–July, when the plants are about 8 in. tall and pencil-thick. Water dry ground before lifting and loosen the roots carefully. Choose strong plants; healthy roots and leaves need no trimming. Make 6 in. deep holes, 6–8 in. apart in rows 12 in. apart. Lower one plant into each hole and water in. Leave the holes to fill naturally.

Alternative: module clumps. From early March, sow three or four seeds per module, ⅝ in. deep. Harden off plants raised under cover and plant while still small, keeping each clump together. Make a hole to take the rootball, firm around it and water in. Allow 12–14 in. between clumps in each direction. The pencil-thick stage and deep individual holes above belong to the single-plant method.

For an earlier crop. Raise individual plants in modules indoors in late winter, in bright conditions above 50°F. Harden off before planting; suitable early plants may go out in April.

Tomato, Outdoor Current book pages 94–95

The source contains sowing, pricking out, potting on and final planting, but the book compresses them into ‘raise seedlings’ and ‘grow on’. A seed-grown route and a bought-plant entry point make the missing stages understandable.

Before 67 words · current planting block

1 Grow on and harden. Raise seedlings under cover in spring or buy sturdy pot-grown plants. Harden off gradually before planting outside.

2 Plant beside support. After frost risk, plant in settled warmth. Water first; keep the rootball intact and set slightly deeper. Support tall types; tie in regularly.

Bush/basket types: use packet spacing; support heavy stems. No routine side-shooting.

Sow under cover in March–early April; plant outside in settled, warm late-spring weather after frost risk.

After — proposed 189 words · +122

Sow under cover. For an outdoor crop, sow indoors in late March–early April, in pots or a seed tray of seed compost. Keep warm for germination and give seedlings plenty of light. Alternatively, buy sturdy plants of an outdoor variety and join the sequence at growing on or hardening off, according to their size.

Prick out, then pot on. When true leaves develop, lift tray-grown seedlings into individual pots, supporting the roots and holding a leaf rather than the stem. Later, move each plant into a larger pot as its roots fill the old one. These are growing-on stages; the plants are still under cover.

Harden off. Gradually get plants used to outdoor conditions over about two weeks, usually during May. Keep them protected from frost.

Plant out. Usually early June in the open, or late May under cloches, after frost risk and in settled warmth. Plants should be well rooted, with the first flowers beginning to open. Water first, keep the rootball intact and plant slightly deeper than its pot level. Space plants 18–24 in. apart, allowing more room for vigorous varieties; follow supplier guidance for compact basket types. Support tall plants and heavy bush stems, and ventilate protective cloches.

Potato Current book pages 76–77

Quick Facts calls tuber planting ‘Sow’. Chitting is named without explanation, first and second earlies share a broad date, and ground, mulch and container methods run together. The existing warning that trench depth is not soil cover must survive.

Before 47 words · current planting block

1 Sprouts upwards. Set seed tubers at trench base, sprouts up.

2 Cover gently. Cover gently; protect sprouts. Water well.

First-early rows. 24 in. between rows

Maincrop rows. 30 in. between rows

Or use holes. No-dig: shallow-plant under thick, light-excluding mulch. Chitting benefits earlies most.

Plant earlies March–April, maincrops April, conditions permitting. Drain pots; give ample compost.

After — proposed 178 words · +131

Prepare seed potatoes. Use healthy certified seed potatoes. Chitting means letting them sprout before planting: stand them with the eyes upwards in a cool, light, frost-free place from midwinter. It is most useful for first earlies.

When to plant. Plant first earlies in late March–April, second earlies in early–mid April and maincrops in mid–late April, when conditions suit. Earlier March planting needs a mild or protected site. Protect emerging shoots from frost.

Plant in the ground. Make trenches about 6 in. deep, or use individual holes. This is the trench depth, not 6 in. of soil above each tuber. Set tubers at the base with sprouts upwards, cover gently and water well. Follow the early/maincrop spacings in Quick Facts; earth up as the stems grow to keep developing tubers covered.

Alternative: under mulch. Set tubers on the soil surface and cover with a thick mulch that excludes light. Keep topping it up so tubers cannot turn green, and check for slugs.

Alternative: containers. Use a large container with drainage. Start tubers in a shallow layer of compost, cover them and add more compost as the stems grow. Keep tubers covered and water regularly.
