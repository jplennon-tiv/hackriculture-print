Garlic is a hardy allium grown from individual cloves, usually planted in autumn for a summer harvest. Flavour
varies with the variety, from mild cloves to strongly pungent types. Young plants tolerate very cold winter
weather, fit conveniently into the garden after summer crops, and are lifted early enough to be followed by
French beans, late brassicas or autumn salads.
Quick facts
Plant cloves Oct–Dec. Jan–Mar for suitable spring
varieties.
Harvest Jun – Aug
Ready in Summer; ~8–9 months from autumn
planting, less from spring
Yield (10 ft row) About 20 ordinary garlic bulbs or
10 elephant garlic bulbs.
Clove depth Cover tips with 1 in. of soil. Plant
deeper only on light ground.
Row spacing 12 in. between rows
Plant spacing Ordinary garlic: 6 in. apart; elephant
garlic: about 12 in. apart

Recommended varieties
SOFTNECK
Solent Wight
A softneck garlic well suited to UK
gardens and storage. A useful first
choice for a dependable household
crop.
SOFTNECK
Germidour
A softneck garlic with purple￾marked skins. A familiar alternative
for gardeners wanting large, useful
bulbs.
Carcassonne Wight
A hardneck garlic providing an
alternative to softneck types. It also
produces edible flower stems,
known as scapes.
LEEK RELATIVE; LARGE MILD CLOVES
Elephant
Produces very large, mild cloves. Grow as a separate
culinary option; it is a leek relative rather than true
garlic.
HARDNECK
Spanish Roja
A strongly flavoured hardneck with thin skins. Remove
tender flower stems as buds form; check specialist
suppliers for planting stock.

White rot affects onions,

rows of small white feeding
