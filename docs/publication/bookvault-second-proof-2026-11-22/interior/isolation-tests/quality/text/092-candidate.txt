Outdoor tomatoes need a warm, sunny spot out of the wind. Choose a variety suited to British summers,
whether you buy young plants or raise your own. In cooler gardens a cloche helps the fruit ripen. Tall cordons
need a support. Bush varieties need less pruning and can grow in pots or under cloches.

Recommended varieties
CORDON
Outdoor Girl
An early cordon tomato suited to
outdoor growing. Provide support
and remove sideshoots as for other
cordon varieties.
CORDON
Gardener's Delight
A familiar cordon tomato with
small red fruits. A useful outdoor
choice in a warm, sheltered
position.
CORDON
Crimson Crush F1
A cordon tomato bred for outdoor
growing with strong late-blight
resistance. Produces large red
fruits for slicing and cooking.
CORDON
Sungold F1
A sweet orange cherry tomato for a sunny, sheltered
outdoor site. Grow as a supported cordon and pick ripe
fruits promptly.
BUSH
Red Alert
An early bush tomato with small red fruits. Useful
where compact plants are preferred to tall, trained
cordons.
Key risks
Blight
Blight causes rapidly
spreading brown leaf and
stem lesions and fruit decay
in damp weather.
Blossom end rot
A dark, leathery patch at the
flower end of the fruit
results from insufficient
calcium reaching developing
tissue, often because root
moisture fluctuates.
Slugs
Slugs chew low-hanging
fruit, particularly on bush
plants.
Split fruit
Ripe fruit may split after
irregular watering or rain
following dry soil.
