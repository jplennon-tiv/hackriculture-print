Growing Techniques - Cultivation

CultivationLifting and using turf

If you are turning a lawn into a vegetable bed and intend to dig it, lift the turf first. It contains useful soil and organic matter, so keep it. You can stack it for later, or bury it as you dig.

Lift it in manageable pieces

Cut the grass short and mark out the bed. With a sharp, square-nosed spade or lawn edging iron, cut strips slightly wider than the spade blade, then cross-cut about twice that width apart.

Start at an edge. Slide the spade under the turf, just below the main mat of roots, and lift each piece. Keep the pieces small enough to handle comfortably. The ground underneath is now ready for digging.

Stack it for later

Make a neat pile with every turf grass-side down. Keep it damp, cover with an opaque tarpaulin and weigh down the edges. Allow at least six months. If it still holds together as turf, leave it longer.

Once it is dark and crumbly, break it up and sieve out stones and any surviving weed roots. This is traditionally called turf loam. Mix it with well-rotted, sieved garden compost for potting, or work it back into a bed.

Or bury it as you dig

If you are double-digging, you can put the turf straight back into the bed. After loosening the subsoil, lay the turves on it grass-side down, then cover with the topsoil from the next trench.

The turf rots beneath the growing layer. Keep the subsoil below the topsoil, as shown in the double-digging steps.

Check for persistent weeds

Remove couch grass runners and other persistent weed roots from the turf and exposed soil. Do not rely on burial or a cool turf stack to kill them.
