Swedes are hardy brassica roots: slower, sweeter, more substantial and much more frost-resistant than turnips.
Orange or yellow flesh and leaves rising from a neck help distinguish them. Cold improves their flavour for
boiling, mashing and seasoning. Allow about a month longer than turnips, with room for roots to swell. June
sowings can follow early lettuce, carrots, broad beans or other early crops.

Recommended varieties
Magres
A purple-topped swede with
yellow flesh and good eating
quality. A strong addition for an
autumn and winter root crop.
Marian
A familiar purple-topped swede
with pale yellow flesh.
Tweed F1
A vigorous British-bred hybrid with
clubroot resistance. Useful where
that disease is a concern, alongside
good rotation and soil care.
Joan
Good-flavoured roots for autumn
cropping. Sow in late spring or
early summer.
Best of All
A hardy, reliable swede with
purple skin and yellow flesh.
Ruby
Sweet, yellow-fleshed roots with
purple skin. Winter-hardy, with
resistance to powdery mildew.
