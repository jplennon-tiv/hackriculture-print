Greenhouse tomatoes offer flavours rarely found in shops. Cordons grow tall: train one main stem up a cane or
string, removing side shoots. Bush types branch naturally; normally leave their side shoots and growing tips
intact. Both need bright light, steady moisture and good ventilation. Pots and growbags dry out quickly.

Recommended varieties
CHERRY AND SMALL FRUITED
Sungold F1
A vigorous cordon tomato with
very sweet orange cherry fruits. A
strong first choice for greenhouse
snacking and salads.
ORDINARY
Alicante
A traditional red salad tomato
grown as a cordon. Retain as an
accessible, general-purpose
greenhouse variety.
F1 HYBRID
Shirley
An early cordon tomato producing
medium red fruits under glass. A
familiar, practical choice for an
unheated greenhouse.
CHERRY AND SMALL FRUITED
Sweet Million F1
A cordon with many small red
cherry fruits: a red-fruited
alternative to orange Sungold.
NOVELTY
San Marzano
A plum tomato for sauces and
cooking rather than fresh salads.
Give it warm, protected conditions.
BUSH
Red Alert
An early bush cherry tomato for
greenhouse beds or pots. Leave
the side shoots to grow.
Key risks
Blight
Tomato blight causes brown
lesions on leaves and stems,
followed by fruit decay and
plant collapse in favourable
damp conditions.
Greenhouse
whitefly
Whitefly adults and
immature stages suck sap;
sticky honeydew can
support black sooty mould.
Blossom end rot
A dark, leathery patch at the
flower end of the fruit
results from insufficient
calcium reaching developing
tissue, often because root
moisture fluctuates.
Split fruit
Irregular watering,
particularly soaking after
severe drying, can split ripe
fruit.
