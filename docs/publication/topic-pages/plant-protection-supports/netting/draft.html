Growing Techniques - Plant care

NettingKeep pests outside

A well-fitted cover can save a crop before the trouble starts. Start with the problem you want to solve, then choose the covering and a framework to hold it in place.

Choose the covering for the job

Open netting

Use bird netting to protect crops from pigeons and other birds. Check the openings: a net that stops birds may let butterflies through. For a permanent enclosure, you can use timber and wire mesh, as in the fruit cage below.

Insect-proof mesh

Fine mesh keeps smaller pests away from crops such as brassicas, carrots and leeks. Choose the grade for the insect you want to exclude; not all mesh is fine enough for every pest. Check the supplier's description.

Horticultural fleece

Fleece provides extra warmth and shelter from cold winds and light frost. It is useful over young plants and early sowings. The protection depends on its weight and the weather; it is not a guarantee against hard frost. Watch for overheating.

A permanent fruit cage

If the crop stays in the same place for years, a permanent cage may be worth the work. Set sturdy timber posts into the ground, join them with rails and brace the corners. Fix galvanised wire mesh securely to the sides and a proper door, and cover the top too: birds will soon find an open roof. You can use lighter bird netting overhead. Allow room for mature bushes and a clear route for pruning and picking, with a door that closes without gaps.

NettingTemporary netting frames

The frame's job is to hold the covering clear of the plants without tearing it. Sharp cane ends, rough corners and projecting fixings are the trouble spots. Keep the surfaces smooth, allow for growth, and choose a system you can open easily.

Bottles over canes

I use canes topped with empty bottles. Push the canes firmly into the ground around the crop and put an intact bottle, neck down, over each top. The covering rests on the smooth bottle bases. It is easy to change the shape or raise the height as plants grow; leave enough spare covering and reseat any loosened canes firmly.

Manufactured frame systems

You can buy modular frames with metal tubes and fitted corner connectors, or hoops with matching covers. The joints enclose the tube ends and make a tidy framework. Follow the maker's assembly and anchoring instructions, check for projecting screws or rough edges, and choose a size that leaves growing room. A fitted cover with an opening can make access easier.

Canes and tennis balls

Old tennis balls can make simple connectors for a light cane frame. Make snug holes for the upright and horizontal canes, then push the ends inside the balls to form the corners. The rounded balls cover the points beneath the netting. Check that the joints grip firmly and add bracing if the frame wobbles. Purpose-made cane connector balls are another option.

Fit the covering to the frame

Keep the material clear of the leaves and secure every edge, using pegs, boards or bricks as appropriate. Leave enough slack for growth and for opening a side, then close it again after access. A light frame still needs firm anchoring.
