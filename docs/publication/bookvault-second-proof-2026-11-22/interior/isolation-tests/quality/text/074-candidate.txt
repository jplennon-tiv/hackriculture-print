Potatoes give heavy crops from small plots; check old grassland for wireworms. Plant seed tubers, not true
seed, and keep new tubers covered: light makes them green and poisonous. Eat first/second earlies fresh.
Maincrops yield and store better, but need more space and face more blight. Give rich soil and moisture; guard
frost-tender leaves. Lift gently; store maincrops cool, dark and frost-free.

Key risks
Blight
Potato blight spreads
rapidly in warm, wet
weather, causing brown
foliage lesions, collapse and
potentially tuber decay.
Slugs
Slugs tunnel into tubers,
particularly in damp soil or
beneath covers and
mulches.
Scab
Common scab causes corky
skin patches and is favoured
by dry conditions as tubers
form, particularly in alkaline
soil.
Eelworm
Potato cyst nematodes can
build up where potatoes are
grown repeatedly.
