# Photorealistic Vegetables

Use John's [allotment harvest proof](../../publication/cover-versions/version-1-1/garden-harvest-natural-pile/Allotment-Harvest.png) as the visual benchmark. Its [source record](../../publication/cover-versions/version-1-1/garden-harvest-natural-pile/ARTWORK.json) identifies the independent upper and lower cutouts and the precise crops from the earlier artwork. Inspect the relevant cutout at native size. The [earlier attempt](../../publication/cover-versions/version-1-1/garden-harvest/Garden-Harvest.png) shows the failure mode: a little soil on three evenly displayed, smoothly bent carrots still reads as staged.

## Direct the pose first

1. Identify the crop, maturity and job of the image. Inspect a credible photographic reference or the accepted original for the relevant growth habit, silhouette, camera angle and how the specimens rest together. When editing a polished trial, use it for crop identity; give the natural reference authority over pose and finish.
2. Describe individual specimens and what bears their weight. For the reference bunch, a short blunt carrot sits in front; a thick shoulder rests against a smaller root; one long taper partly hides another. Specify comparable crop-specific relationships: a bean pod twisting onto its seam, a pumpkin settling on its flattened flank, a cabbage leaf buckling under the cut head. Vary girth, maturity, orientation, spacing and visibility. Let overlaps conceal parts of the crop.
3. Add a few local signs of growing and handling. Place caught earth at a root crown, a dry flap on a shallot, a snapped petiole or a nick on an outer leaf. Keep freshly cut flesh, peas and inner leaves clean. Avoid blanket dots, repeated curves, equal tapers, graduated rows, evenly spaced tips and deliberate damage on every specimen.
4. Light the group from one direction. For the cover, render the header and footer independently on transparent backgrounds, preserve their outer silhouettes, and place each as a replaceable layer. Use the cast-shadow workflow below. Save each revision separately.
5. Judge the silhouette at thumbnail size before inspecting skin detail. Can you see which specimen rests in front, where the weight falls and which shapes differ? At native size check attachments, cut surfaces, stray or severed outlines, noise and transparency. At placement size check the title, badge overlaps and visible tips. If the pose looks staged, redraw its construction before adding surface texture.

For a photographic interior illustration, use the same anatomy and light, with the framing required by its purpose. Keep the teaching feature visible. Refer to approved content for an instructional detail and use the appropriate layout workflow for placement. The existing graphic hero, icon and schematic styles continue under their own contracts. Record the prompt, references, source artwork and proof status; each new composition is a proposal.

## Cast shadows from a harvest group

Use the [tomato truss study](../../publication/cover-versions/version-1-1/tomato-truss-light-study/README.md) as John's accepted lighting reference. Compare it with the [original cream artwork](../../publication/cover-versions/version-1-1/layered-master/sources/cream-trial-before-restoration.png) at native size. Check both the dark places where a vegetable touches the wood and the small windows of sunlight between specimens.

- Trace the objects that intercept the light, including shapes inside the group's outer silhouette. Select actual branch forks, leaflets, pod edges, dry roots or curled leaf margins from the photograph. Identify the surface beneath each one. A raised branch can shade a fruit, disappear behind it, and cast an identifiable shape farther away on the board.
- Follow one light direction across the arrangement. In the truss proof the cast travels down and left on the board. Keep a grounded tip close to its shadow. Give raised foliage more separation and a softer edge. Keep occasional sharp contacts among the softer overlapping shadows. Judge these distances from the pose and the light.
- Preserve grain in shaded wood and detail on vegetable skins. Keep existing photographic shading between specimens. Add a restrained, surface-clipped shadow where an identifiable caster is missing. Broad bodies and fine foliage can contribute to the same shadow without making the overlap twice as dark.
- Keep board shadows beneath the photograph and surface-specific shading above it within the replaceable harvest group. When the crop or pose changes, derive the cast from the new artwork. If the original layout corresponds closely, sample the light on visible wood for calibration, masking out lettering, stickers, soil specks and vegetable pixels.
- Compare a crop at native size and then the whole cover. Check the light in gaps, contact at the base and readability of fine casts at book size. Verify that accepted regions and fixed components survive the edit.
