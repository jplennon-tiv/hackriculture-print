What the research supports

The canonical pea and broad-bean guides already say to cut spent plants at ground level and retain their nitrogen-rich roots. The kale record permits following an early crop and adding mature compost where needed. These exact methods and the existing sowing/planting timing remain the starting point.

John’s manuscript places leafy brassicas after legumes in the four-block system. His June allotment account describes leeks following overwintered broad beans and explains the nitrogen benefit. The local transcript is machine-generated; no uncertain crop names or quantified yield claims are used.

UConn’s pea guide and AHDB’s explanation of rhizobia agree that bacteria in root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen. Nitrogen in retained plant material becomes available as that material decomposes. The amount and timing vary.

AHDB’s rotation guidance supports a residual benefit to following crops. RHS pea advice also retains roots after harvest. These support “some nitrogen” and “help feed”, without a promise that the roots supply every nutrient the next crop needs.

Garden Organic’s field-bean green manure returns the whole crop before seed formation. That is a different use from harvesting beans and leaving roots: its full nitrogen-return benefit is not assumed for this example. No green-manure waiting period, arable fertiliser rate or US planting date has been imported.
