Globe artichoke is a large, handsome thistle-family perennial grown for its unopened flower buds, unlike the
underground tubers harvested from Jerusalem or Chinese artichokes. It is decorative enough for a border, with
grey-green fern-like leaves and strong stems, but it needs more space than most vegetables and is not a natural
choice for a very small garden. The reward is a distinctive, fine-flavoured crop: pick the heads while they are still
tight and tender, before the scales open and the centre becomes tough. The plants establish quickly in rich,
moist but well-drained soil, though they need regular watering, feeding, mulching and some protection in cold
winters.

Recommended varieties
Green Globe
A familiar green-headed artichoke for a permanent
sunny bed. Harvest the flower buds while their scales
remain tightly closed.
Gros Vert De Laon
A hardy, traditional green artichoke with substantial
heads. A sensible alternative for a permanent
vegetable garden.
Imperial Star
A seed-grown selection producing generous green
heads. A useful addition for gardeners establishing an
artichoke bed from seed.
Purple Globe
Purple-tinted heads add colour to the plot and kitchen.
