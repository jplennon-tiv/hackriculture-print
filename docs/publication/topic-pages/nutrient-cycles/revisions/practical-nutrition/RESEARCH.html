Checks against online sources

  • AHDB — Biological fixation of nitrogen by rhizobia — Root-nodule bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen; residues release nitrogen through mineralisation; contribution varies.
  • RHS — Organic matter: how to use in the garden — Photosynthesis, plant residues, decomposition and carbon dioxide; organic matter binds soil particles, retains nutrients and moisture; some forms last longer.
  • Garden Organic — The function of plant nutrients — Pages 2–4: organic and mineral phosphorus pools, availability and root function; potassium pools, water balance, fruit development, compost/comfrey recycling; other nutrients and uptake conditions.
  • RHS — How plants absorb nutrients — All plants need nutrients; nitrogen supports leafy growth, phosphorus roots, potassium fruiting; carbon comes from air.
  • RHS — Seaweed products — Seaweed provides potassium and trace elements; most soils have adequate micronutrients. Collection needs permission. Amounts are variable, so no complete-feed guarantee.
  • Garden Organic — Growing comfrey — Permanent undisturbed patch; let plants establish before regular cutting.
  • Garden Organic — Comfrey harvesting and use — Leaves can be mixed into compost to recycle their nutrients.
  • RHS — Three ways to use comfrey — Bocking 14 and composting cut leaves: a compact useful method without a liquid-feed recipe.

Copy and composition choices

All crops need the listed nutrients; examples indicate particular benefits. Legumes capture new atmospheric nitrogen, while compost and comfrey recycle existing material.
