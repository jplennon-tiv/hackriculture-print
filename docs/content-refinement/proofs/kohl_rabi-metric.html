Kohl Rabi.

DIFFICULTYEasy1/5
HARVEST SEASONSSummer / Autumn

Kohl rabi looks like a root crop, but the edible globe is really a swelling of the stem just above ground level. It is a cabbage relative with a sweet, mild brassica flavour and a delicate texture when grown quickly and picked young. The crop matures fast, often in about 8-12 weeks, making it useful as a half-season brassica. Quality depends on steady moisture and timely harvesting: drought, checks to growth or leaving the globes too long can make them hard and woody. Small young globes are good raw or cooked, while larger late varieties can stand into autumn if they keep growing steadily.

Recommended varieties

Green Vienna

Green-skinned, white-fleshed - an early maturing variety chosen for spring and summer sowing. Sometimes listed in catalogues as White Vienna.

Lanro

An open-pollinated variety with pale green globes and white flesh. Harvest young for tender texture.

Purple Vienna

Purple-skinned globes with white flesh. Useful for late crops; choose sowing dates for the local season and harvest while tender.

Rowel

A named kohl rabi selection. Keep moisture steady and harvest while tender; do not assume oversized globes will remain free of woodiness.

White Superschmelz

A useful late or second-sowing variety noted for making larger globes without quickly turning woody; can be sown as late as July for late autumn use.

Key risks

Woody Kohl Rabi

Drought, growth checks, crowding or delayed harvesting make the swollen stem hard and fibrous.

Flea Beetle

Tiny jumping beetles chew small round holes in young leaves, especially in warm dry weather.

Cabbage Caterpillars

Caterpillars of cabbage white butterfly, cabbage moth and related pests chew holes in leaves.

Club Root (Finger and Toe)

Swollen, distorted roots cause poor growth and wilting in sunshine.

Soil & preparation

  1. Choose a sunny site with fertile, well-drained soil. Test soil before adding lime.
  2. It is more dependent on steady moisture than its tough appearance suggests. Dry soil or checks to growth make the swollen stem hard and woody.
  3. Prepare the bed in good time, adding mature compost where needed. Give roots room to develop and maintain steady moisture; the edible globe is a swollen stem above ground.

Sowing & planting

1 Sow shallowly

Sow thinly where plants will crop. Cover lightly; start in April and sow in succession through early summer, then again in July for a late crop.

2 Thin young plants

Start at the first true leaves and thin gradually to final spacing. Keep plants growing steadily; water in dry weather to avoid woody stems.

Plant hardened modules before they exceed 5 cm tall, at 15 cm in rows 30 cm apart. Allow more room for giant varieties.

Looking after the crop

  1. Thin promptly. Protect young plants from birds; use collars or mesh where root fly is a problem.
  2. Weed carefully. Check moisture and pests if growth slows; use an organic feed where fertility is poor.
  3. Water during dry spells to prevent woody globes. Allow more room for larger autumn crops. Steady growth matters: dry checks make swollen stems tough and woody. Autumn crops also need enough room as they grow more slowly in falling temperatures.

Harvesting & storage

  1. Pull the swollen stem bases or globes while they are still young and tender, from about golf-ball size up to roughly tennis-ball size. Do not wait for extra growth if the variety is not intended for it, because the flesh becomes woody.
  2. Late crops can stand through autumn and, in mild areas, into December. Protect with fleece or cloches before wintry weather and harvest while still tender.
  3. The best quality is usually from fresh-pulled globes. They may be kept briefly in cool conditions, but they deteriorate once lifted.
