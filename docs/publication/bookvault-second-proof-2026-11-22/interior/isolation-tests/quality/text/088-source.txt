Sweet corn needs an early variety suited to local summers and a warm, sunny, sheltered site. Full-sized cobs
need wind pollination: plant a block of short rows. Expect one main cob per plant, sometimes a smaller second.
Baby corn is picked before pollination and can grow in rows.

Recommended varieties
SUPERSWEET
Swift F1
Early, compact corn with tender, sweet kernels for
short UK summers.
SUPERSWEET
Earlibird F1
Early supersweet hybrid for ripe cobs in comparatively
short summers.
TENDERSWEET
Lark F1
Mid-season tendersweet with soft, sweet kernels,
extending early harvests.
BABY CORN
Minipop F1
Baby corn: pick small, immature cobs; a separate use
from full-sized sweetcorn.
Key risks
Slugs
Slugs damage young plants
most when cold or wet
conditions slow growth.
Cold check
Cold soil and weather slow
growth and nutrient uptake;
seedlings may turn yellow
and stall.
Poor pollination
Gaps in full-sized cobs
commonly result from poor
pollination.
Cross pollination
Pollen from maize or
different sweet-corn types
can reduce the sweetness or
tenderness of supersweet
cobs.
