Endive is a cool-season salad plant with more flavour and bitterness than lettuce, though less of lettuce's
crispness. It can be eaten raw in mixed salads or cooked, and both curly-leaved frisée types and broader-leaved
Batavian or escarole types are useful. Curly endive is usually for late-summer and autumn cutting, while broad￾leaved endive is easier to grow for good hearts and is more useful in autumn and winter. The crop needs
moisture, humus and some protection from heat or harsh frost; blanching, or choosing self-blanching varieties,
gives paler, milder hearts. Small late-summer sowings can provide leaves and hearts into winter, with suitable
protection in cold weather.

Key risks
Slugs
Slugs can damage seedlings
and blanched endive hearts
in cool, damp weather.
Bolting
Cold while young, drought
and other growth checks can
cause endive to flower
prematurely.
Rotting
Pale hearts can rot quickly in
damp conditions.
Frost
Hard frost can damage
hearts.
