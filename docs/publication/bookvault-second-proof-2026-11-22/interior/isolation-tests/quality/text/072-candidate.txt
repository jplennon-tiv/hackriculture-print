Peas are a reward of early summer: freshly picked pods are sweet, but sugars soon turn to starch. Although long
grown for canning, freezing and drying, home-grown peas taste best picked young and eaten or cooked at once.
The names round and wrinkled describe the dry seed in the packet. Round-seeded varieties are generally
hardier, making them useful for early crops; wrinkled varieties are usually sweeter.

Key risks
Pea moth /
caterpillars
The most obvious pea
problem is caterpillars inside
pods, eating the peas.
Mildew
Mildew often appears from
about the second week of
July onwards, especially on
late sowings or in damp,
muggy weather.
Birds
Birds eat seed and seedlings
and may peck leaves.
Mice
Mice may take the large
edible seeds before
germination.
