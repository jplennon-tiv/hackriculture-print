Making compostthe secret’s in the bin

Composting keeps useful material in the garden. Last year’s plant waste becomes part of next year’s growing medium. The important bit is giving it somewhere convenient to break down.

Filling

Add new material here as it arrives.

Rotting

Leave the full bin to break down.

Ready

Keep finished compost until needed.

Three bins is best

With one bin, you will always be in a mess. With two bins, you may need to bag up finished compost to make room. Three bins let each job have its own space. You do not have to shovel the contents along a row: the bins change jobs as they become empty.

Buy one or make one

For a bought bin, look for a sound lid, sturdy sides, a secure rodent-resistant base and an opening you can actually empty through. Bigger bins are best; they retain heat and moisture better. A second matching bin is often more useful than a clever gadget attached to the first.

Protect the base

Lay galvanised mesh with 3⁄16 in openings over the soil. Overlap the brick footing and weight the edges; in timber bins, turn the mesh up and screw battens over its edges. Keep a few small vents: four short ⅝ in pipes through brick joints, or small mesh-covered holes in timber sides.

Use wooden lids

Screw planks to two battens underneath, overlapping the bin edges. Hinge to the back rail and fit a secure lid stay. Nail roofing felt over the top, fold it over the edges and trap it with thin timber strips. A fixed rear strip makes the lifting part lighter; overlap the hinge joint with flexible rubber to shed rain.

Brick: Bricks, bagged concrete and mortar, a reinforced concrete lintel, decking boards, masonry fixings, hinges and a latch. Timber: 3 in square posts, stout outdoor boards, battens, exterior screws, hinges and a latch. Use timber suitable for damp outdoor conditions.

Build your ownbrick or timber

Choose level, well-drained ground with working room in front. A bay about 3 ft 3 in wide, deep and high inside is a useful starting point. Mark the wall positions and check the diagonals match. Each drawing shows one bay: repeat it to make your set.

A brick bin

A timber bin

Strip footing · front door · lintel

Corner posts · lift-out front boards

1Pour the footing

On firm ground, dig a strip 8–12 in deep around each bay, at least twice a brick’s width or a spade-width, whichever is wider. Leave the middle as soil. Pour concrete, level it and let it set.

2Build the walls

Lay bricks in mortar with staggered joints; check level and upright as you go. Leave a front opening. Seat the lintel above it with the bearing specified by its supplier. Build no more than five courses in a day.

3Finish the top and door

Build the back higher than the front. Form a smooth slope with mortar between clamped boards, then fix timber capping after it sets. Hinge a plank door with two cross rails and a diagonal from the lower hinge to the opposite upper corner. Fit a latch.

1Make the frame

Set four posts per bay, with about 12 in below ground; backfill and ram firmly. Adjacent bays can share posts. Screw close-set boards to the backs, sides and divisions. Predrill screw holes near board ends.

2Leave the front removable

Fix two upright battens to each front post to form a channel. Cut front boards to slide down between them. Leave a little clearance so damp boards will still lift out. Test one before cutting the rest.

3Fit the lid rails

Set the back rail higher than the front so the lid sheds rain. Fix side rails to support its edges. Check the frame is square and firm before fitting the lid; tighten loose joints before adding compost.
