# Evidence and editorial decisions

2 November 2026. John requested broad online research for this topic rather than
the usual research-skill workflow. The exact vegetable records were subsequently
read through the supported shared reader, and the crop changes checked against
the current book. No canonical advice or pH values changed.

The current brief is **one page at the end of Organic growing**, with a short
introduction and two panels. It replaces the longer first draft, which John found
coherent but too much for readers to take in. The new body has 218 words.

## Evidence retained in the page

| Advice | Evidence and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Meaning of pH; broad vegetable range | [RHS pH/testing](https://www.rhs.org.uk/soil-composts-mulches/ph-and-testing-soil) explains acid below 7, neutral at 7 and alkaline above. [Garden Organic](https://www.gardenorganic.org.uk/expert-advice/garden-management/soil/managing-your-soil) supports understanding and working with the soil. The introduction gives a general 6–7 range, not a universal precise target. The separate scale is omitted. |
| When and how to test | RHS, the [Garden Organic activity booklet](https://gardenorganic-assets.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/documents/Testing-soil-and-improving-fertility.pdf), and [Gardman instructions](https://www.gardenhealth.com/soil-ph-testing-kit) support representative samples, testing distinct areas separately, following the particular kit and reading against its own chart. The page retains those essentials without prescribing universal depths, ratios, water types or settling times. |
| Doubtful kit readings | John reports mixed results with bought kits. [South Dakota State Extension’s comparison](https://extension.sdstate.edu/are-cheap-home-garden-soil-test-kits-accurate) found variable performance; it is not a UK buying guide. Repeating a doubtful reading is a practical precaution, not a guarantee of accuracy. Laboratory confirmation remains excluded at John’s express request. |
| Waiting after additions | RHS advises against testing within three months of lime, fertiliser or organic matter. This interval remains visible in the testing panel. It is not a claim that all surface-applied lime finishes reacting in three months. |
| Garden lime and conditional use | [RHS liming](https://www.rhs.org.uk/soil-composts-mulches/lime-liming) identifies ground limestone/calcium carbonate. [Soil Association fertility guidance](https://www.soilassociation.org/media/4349/7-rotations-and-fertility-2013.pdf) includes liming when needed in organic growing. The page explains its purpose and rejects routine annual application. It does not generalise Dowding’s approach to all organic gardens. |
| Quantity, timing and handling | RHS explains that the amount depends on the soil, starting pH and product, and that surface application is slower. The page requires the packet’s appropriate rate, even application and handling instructions. Soil Association guidance supports keeping lime separate from manure to avoid nitrogen loss. No universal dose or automatic follow-up treatment is invented. |
| Crop exceptions | [RHS clubroot](https://www.rhs.org.uk/disease/club-root) says liming acid soil can reduce but not eliminate the disease. [RHS potato scabs](https://www.rhs.org.uk/disease/potato-scabs) supports avoiding lime before potatoes. Both cautions remain in one concise paragraph. |

## Intentional condensation

The first draft’s discussion of dolomitic lime and builders’ lime, clay versus
sandy-soil quantities, the extended no-dig/retesting explanation, detailed sample
preparation and water specification, extra compost panel, and John’s ten-year
account are omitted. The main text simply specifies garden lime and the chosen
product’s directions. The two existing illustrations are retained unchanged;
their prompts and sources remain in ARTWORK.json.

Underlying research still supports compost care without claiming it guarantees
a suitable pH: [Oregon State Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/ec-1561-improving-garden-soils-organic-matter)
distinguishes organic-matter benefits from correcting excessive acidity. The
preceding research found no organic-gardening consensus against testing or lime;
Garden Organic and the Soil Association still include appropriate correction.
Those broader debates are outside this short practical page. No new horticultural
claim was introduced in condensing the researched draft.
