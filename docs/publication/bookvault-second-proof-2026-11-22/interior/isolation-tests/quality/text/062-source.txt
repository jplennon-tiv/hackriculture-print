Quick-growing lettuce gives repeat leaves or hearts in cool, moist weather, spring–autumn. Slugs love young
plants; heat/drought encourage bolting and tipburn. Sow hearting types in small batches to avoid a glut.
Quick facts
Germination
6–12 days
Sowing depth
Outdoors: 1/2 in.;
trays: cover lightly.
Harvest
May – Oct
Yield (10 ft row)
10-20 heads
Ready in
From sowing: loose leaves 6–8 weeks; hearts
8–14 weeks
Sow Mar–Aug in batches. Feb under cover. Sow
suitable overwintering varieties Aug–early Sep.
Plant out Mar–Aug. Protect early plants with fleece
or cloches.
Row spacing Outdoor seed rows: 12 in.; final
spacing by type.
Plant spacing Hearting: 12 in.; loose-leaf: 6 in.
Check packet for compact types.

Recommended varieties
COS
Little Gem
Compact, crisp, sweet cos hearts
for small gardens and repeat
sowings.
LOOSE-LEAF
Salad Bowl
Loose leaves for small, repeated
pickings without harvesting a
whole head.
COS
Winter Density
Compact, cooler-season lettuce;
use protection to extend salad
harvests.
BUTTERHEAD
All The Year Round
Familiar butterhead for successive
sowings; a soft-leaved alternative
to cos and loose-leaf.
LOOSE-LEAF
Lollo Rossa
Frilled red loose-leaf for colour and
texture. Pick outer leaves or take
whole young plants.
CRISPHEAD
Webb’s Wonderful
Large, frilly, crunchy hearts for
summer cropping. More crinkled
than true iceberg types.
Key risks
Slugs
Ragged leaves and slime
trails are signs of slugs.
Root aphid
Plants may suddenly wilt
and die as soil-dwelling
aphids attack roots.
Bolting
Lettuces produce thick
flowering stems if left in the
soil after hearts have
formed.
Downy mildew
Older leaves, especially in
damp autumn weather, may
develop mildew.
