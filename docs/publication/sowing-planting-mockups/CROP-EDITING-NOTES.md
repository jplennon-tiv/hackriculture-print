# Working guidance for Sowing & planting

Apply these notes as each vegetable comes up for review. The approved examples are [carrot](carrot-revision/REVIEW.html) and [leek](leek-revision/REVIEW.html). The [other three examples](remaining-three/APPROVAL.json) are also approved, including Beetroot’s amended storage wording. The [39-guide rollout](../sowing-rollout/REVIEW.html) is the current review.

- Use **Sow** for putting seed in the ground. Use **Sow** in Quick facts and planting instructions, and **SOW** in the calendar. Reserve a qualifier for a real choice the reader needs to make, such as starting seed indoors. Keep planting out seedlings, transplanting from a nursery bed and planting tubers distinct.
- Use **Transplant** in Quick facts and **TRANSPLANT** in the calendar for the same operation. Use the planting icon alongside the fact. This applies when seedlings are lifted and replanted, as on leek. Keep any separate sowing, growing-on and planting-out stages clear.
- Put calendar dates in Quick facts or the calendar. Keep the **Extended season** entry short, with the month and the protection required for that crop. Carrot trials the book's existing winter snowflake beside this entry. March, August and the choice of cover are specific to carrot.
  John's crop-specific exception for Jerusalem Artichoke also repeats the planting months and wet-soil qualification in **Plant tubers**, using available space in the practical panel. This does not reinstate duplicated dates across the set.
- Keep separate planting steps short. Preserve measurements, site conditions and the information needed to carry out each step. Keep readiness and durations with the relevant action.
- When John requests measurement rounding, choose values that are easy to remember and measure independently in each unit. His 5 November Celery decision is **3 ft / 1 m**: keep the convenient metric figure rather than mechanically converting the rounded imperial value. Preserve crop context and existing safety/biological limits; canonical changes still need the authorised guarded write route.
- When thinning is a later care job, check the existing **Looking after the crop** advice first. Give the instruction once where it belongs, including any important spacing or pest precaution. Carrot keeps its three-picture sequence as a visual reminder.
- Give conventional planting instructions on the crop pages. Clump growing is reserved for a future Techniques page.
- Aim for a facing pair. Try column widths with the existing body type and check the actual spread in both units. Let each crop's content determine the useful split.
- As each crop is reviewed, actively improve the **Quick facts** arrangement. Group short values beside longer qualifications and adjust column proportions for comfortable wrapping. Preserve useful facts, icons and body type. The [approved Globe Artichoke amendment](../sowing-rollout/revisions/artichoke_globe/APPROVAL.json) is the current example; use crop-specific arrangements rather than a fixed grid for every crop.
- Remove routine **pH and liming** references from vegetable instructions unless there is a definite crop-specific need. A general preference or a numeric target alone is insufficient. Retain an essential, evidenced warning or conditional treatment where it matters; explain the relevant condition and never imply routine liming. Check the full crop spread as each comes up for review. The Techniques pH page remains the home for the general explanation. This 5 November direction supersedes the earlier policy of retaining routine pH targets or merely adding “test first”.
  John explicitly retains Asparagus's existing “lime very acid ground if needed” wording in the current review.
- Give the practical title room above the reminder banner and restore the crop’s current **See also** destinations before judging page fit. In an illustrated sequence, align the feature that the pictures share, such as the soil surface. Keep the captions on a common baseline.
- For a crowded bound-book vegetable guide, start with the complete selected **Pests & diseases** table. Prune unnecessary wording first. Try the advice and table column widths with the whole table, title, cross-references and normal spacing in place. If either edition still overflows, remove the bottom entry and remeasure. Stop as soon as both fit. Preserve the order and advice of the retained entries and record each print omission.

The approved three-spread review uses **Sow indoors / Plant out** for pot-raised outdoor tomatoes and **Chit / Plant tubers** for potatoes. Protected dates are qualified in Quick facts.
