Asparagus is one of the finest spring vegetables: a long-lived perennial which gives a fresh, delicate crop just
when the garden is short of new green shoots. It is not difficult once established, but it asks for space, clean
ground and patience. Treat the bed as a permanent feature: clear perennial weeds thoroughly, improve the soil
before planting and do not crop hard while the crowns are young. In return, a good bed can produce spears for
many years, followed by tall ferny summer growth which feeds the roots for the next season.

Recommended varieties
Gijnlim
An early, all-male hybrid producing
green spears. A strong first choice
for a productive, permanent
asparagus bed.
Connover's Colossal
A long-established asparagus with
substantial green spears. Keep as
the traditional alternative to
modern all-male hybrids.
Backlim
An all-male hybrid cropping later
than early selections. Plant
alongside an earlier variety to
spread the asparagus harvest.
Pacific Purple
Tender purple spears with a sweet
flavour. The colour fades with
cooking; try them lightly steamed
or raw in salads.
Mondeo
An all-male hybrid with good
yields of green spears, especially
early in the season. A useful
alternative to Gijnlim.
Guelph Millennium
A cold-tolerant, all-male Canadian
hybrid producing green spears. A
useful option for a colder garden,
provided the soil drains well.
Key risks
Violet root rot
Purple fungal growth on
roots, with yellowing and
dying fern, can indicate
violet root rot.
Frost
Young shoots may turn black
and die if a severe frost
occurs in late spring.
Perennial weeds
Couch grass, bindweed and
other perennial weeds are a
serious long-term problem
in asparagus beds.
Slugs
Spears are gnawed, making
them unfit for table use.
