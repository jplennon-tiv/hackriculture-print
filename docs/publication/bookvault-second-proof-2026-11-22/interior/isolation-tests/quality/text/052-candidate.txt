Florence fennel, or bulb fennel, is a sweet, aniseed-flavoured umbellifer grown for the swollen bases of its leaf￾stalks. It belongs to the same broad family as carrot, parsnip and celeriac, but is eaten quite differently: sliced
raw in salads, cooked gently for a soft creamy flavour, or used in soups and ratatouille. The feathery leaves are
also edible, with a gentle aniseed flavour. Modern varieties have made it easier to grow by reducing the
tendency to bolt before useful bulbs form. Match the variety to its sowing season and keep young plants
growing steadily: drought, crowding or root checks can give stemmy plants instead of juicy bulbs. Summer
sowings after an early crop are useful for autumn harvesting. Harvest while the bulbs are tender, before they
become fibrous, stemmy or frosted.
