A FEW DETAILS WORTH KNOWING

How to use
these sheets.

The symbols and shorthand make the guides quick to scan. Here are the distinctions that are less obvious at first glance.

Reading the calendar shades

Sow · usual months
Sow · less usual months
Harvest · usual months
Harvest · less usual months

Brighter marks show the usual window; muted marks show less usual possibilities, such as earlier or later crops. They are not a promise of a longer season. Variety, protection and local conditions matter. A blank month has no timing marked in this guide.

The sowing row can include months for starting under glass. Read the planting method before treating a marked month as suitable for sowing outdoors. Season badges describe the main harvest season, not frost hardiness.

Two scales, different meanings

Difficulty runs from 1 (easier) to 5 (harder). Core Needs runs from 1 (low) to 5 (high) for light, water and nutrition. A high water score is a relative need, not a watering frequency or quantity. Follow the crop's care advice for what to do.

Spacing is about the final crop

Read “between rows”, “between plants” and “each way” separately. A sowing or nursery spacing may differ from the final spacing after thinning or transplanting. Keep any crop-type, clump or container qualification with its measurement.

When does the clock start?

“Ready in” may count from sowing, planting or another stated stage. Use that starting point, rather than comparing the numbers alone. The time to a first harvest and the calendar's harvest season describe different things.

Compare yields on the same basis

A yield may be per plant or for a stated length of row. Check the label before estimating your own harvest. Choose the metric or imperial edition you prefer and keep the measurement with its context; the figures are practical guides, not guarantees.

From a warning to a diagnosis

Key Risks highlights selected crop problems. The vegetable table gives a quick reference; the fuller Troubles guides separate recognition, immediate action and prevention. Read the crop-applicability label: a shared guide's conditions do not all affect every crop. Compare symptoms as well as the picture.

Colour helps you find your way

Page colours identify the collection's growing groups. They are not a crop-rotation plan or a botanical family tree: for example, Kohl Rabi appears with Root Crops. Calendar and Core Needs signal colours keep the same meaning across the groups.

Keep the context beside the number. Varieties, growing methods and local conditions can change timing and spacing. Where a short fact needs qualification, the planting and care text supplies it. Each guide is numbered separately; use the contents titles to find the matching document.
