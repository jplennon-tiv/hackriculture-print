Sweet peppers and chillies are colourful, heat-loving relatives of tomatoes and potatoes. Perennials in their
native climate, they are tender annuals in Britain, most reliable under glass or in warm shelter. Green peppers
are edible but immature. Ripening changes their colour and, in many varieties, makes them sweeter or hotter
with deeper flavour. Fully coloured fruit take longer and slow new fruit production: choose between an earlier,
heavier green crop and fewer, later ripe peppers.

Recommended varieties
Redskin
Compact sweet pepper for pots or
small greenhouses; green fruit
ripens red.
California Wonder
Familiar blocky sweet pepper. Pick
green or ripen red in warm
protection.
Corno di Toro Rosso
Long, tapering, thin-skinned red
sweet peppers for cooking; an
alternative to bells.
Demon Red
Compact chilli for pots, with small,
hot fruit. Give warmth and shelter.
Habanero Orange
Very hot orange chilli needing a
long, warm growing season.
Gypsy F1
An early sweet pepper with pale
green fruits that ripen red. Give it
warmth, regular watering and
support when laden.
Key risks
Poorfruit set
Flowers may fail to set
during cold, excessive heat
or drought.
Red spider mite
Sweet peppers can develop
pale mottling and bronzing
when red spider mites feed
beneath leaves.
Blossom end rot
Brown patches appear at
the bottom of the fruit - a
frequent problem where
growing bags are used.
