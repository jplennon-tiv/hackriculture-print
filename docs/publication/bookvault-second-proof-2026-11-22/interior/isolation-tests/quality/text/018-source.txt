French beans give crisp young pods, fresh shelled flageolets or dried haricots. Much less hardy than broad
beans, they need warm soil but then grow quickly and crop generously. Dwarfs crop first and need no formal
supports; low pods risk mud splash and slugs. Climbers need a frame, but keep pods off the ground, reduce
bending and crop longer. Pods can be round or flat, green, yellow or purple; purple ones usually turn green
when cooked.

Key risks
Black bean aphid
Black bean aphids can
cluster on French-bean
shoots in summer.
No pods
French beans may drop
flowers or set few pods
when roots are dry,
especially in hot weather.
Pea and bean
weevil
Tell-tale signs are U-shaped
notches around the edges of
young pea or bean leaves,
and sometimes damage to
the growing centres.
Birds
Pigeons can eat seedlings
and peck young leaves.
Crows may dig up seeds or
pull out young plants.
