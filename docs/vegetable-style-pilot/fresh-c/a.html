CARROTS / FROM SEED TO SUPPER

Grow it.
Love it.

Your growing companion

A little care goes a long way.

01

Make a good seedbed.

Use deep, fertile, sandy, stone-free soil; choose short roots for heavy or stony ground. Avoid fresh manure just before sowing.

02

Sow into warm soil.

Carrots are slow to germinate, especially in cold soil. Early sowing is most reliable in a dry spring; in cold, wet weather it is often better to wait until the soil has warmed. Water dry drills before sowing and keep germinating seed moist.

03

Thin with care.

Thin to 5–8 cm in damp weather, firm soil and destroy thinnings. Weed carefully while seedlings are small and avoid disturbing mature roots.

04

Keep moisture steady.

Water deeply and evenly; sudden soaking after drought can split roots. Keep young beds bare and sow later crops after clearing early weeds.

THE GOOD BIT

Pull. Crunch. Enjoy.

Pull small carrots as required from June onwards. Baby roots can be ready about 8 weeks after sowing, but avoid pulling at random along the row because disturbed roots can attract carrot fly. Work from one end of the row and ease roots out with a fork if the soil is hard.

October is the usual time to lift maincrop carrots for storage. Maincrop roots can remain in the ground for a while in mild conditions, but growth slows in late autumn and slug or maggot damage becomes more likely. Lift before hard frost, before very wet weather causes splitting, or earlier if carrot fly damage is seen.

Save some for later.

Store only sound trimmed roots in dry sand, cool and just above freezing.

Cut leaves to about 1.3 cm above the crowns. Keep roots apart and check stored carrots occasionally, removing any that rot.

SPOT IT / SORT IT

Keep an eye out.

Carrot Fly

Tunnelled roots. Use fine mesh; remove thinnings; avoid bruising.

Fanging

Forked roots Remove stones, improve soil deeply and avoid fresh manure.

Small Roots

Small roots; weak growth. Improve soil; use organic feed if growth is poor.

Splitting

Split roots. Water evenly; mulch in drought; use split roots now.

One last thing.

Mix seed with dry sand if useful; cover lightly with fine soil. Pelleted seed can be sown about 2.5 cm apart, then thinned to the final spacing if needed.
