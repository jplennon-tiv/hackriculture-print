# Choosing a camera position

[Open the local picture board](../../../assets/garden-media-library/local-references/index.html) for full-size files, or use the [illustrated viewpoint atlas](../../../assets/garden-media-library/local-references/viewpoint-atlas/Garden-and-Allotment-Viewpoints.pdf). The 118 stills cover 62 home-garden views and 56 allotment views, including two reserved comparison pictures. Each ID resolves to a local file. For example, `python3 scripts/resolve_refs.py A55 A28 A50` gives the paving outside the open allotment shed, a view looking out across it, and the threshold detail.

Choose the site and period first. Pick the picture closest to the proposed camera position, then a second view that establishes where the fixed features sit. Use the first picture for the framing. Use the second to check which parts of the background the subject would hide. An oblique and a close-up often answer different questions. The stills register gives the exact seek in the original camera clip or in the published edit. Published dates identify the episode. Match the contents of the picture to dated camera evidence before using it for a particular season or year.

## Where the evidence runs out

The rear garden and front garden each have photographed viewpoints. The present continuous walk between them, and a ground-level reverse along the house side passage, are gaps in the retained set. The allotment has a strong shed-to-beds sequence. Short stretches beneath the shed roof, foliage and containers are still approximate in Working Map 03. The historical studies and the dated seasonal close-ups should be used at their documented period. For an unphotographed reverse, choose the nearest supported angle or identify what a new photograph needs to show. Keep H09 and A03 available for independent comparison.
