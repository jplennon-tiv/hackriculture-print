Jerusalem artichokes are not true artichokes at all, but vigorous sunflower relatives grown for their knobbly
underground tubers. They are hardy, reliable and useful for winter soups, stews and grated salads, with a sweet,
nutty flavour which is not to everybody's taste and may not suit every digestion. The plants grow very tall in rich
soil, making them useful as a screen or windbreak but also capable of robbing light from lower crops if planted
in a long row. They are easy to grow from tubers, but hard to eradicate once established because even small
pieces left in the soil can regrow.

Beetroot’s sweet, earthy roots suit raw salads, hot dishes, pickles, borsch and chutneys; young leaves are edible
too. This cool-climate crop needs well-drained, humus-rich soil and steady moisture. Growth starts slowly, then
roots swell quickly. Sow small batches or raise module clumps. Early sowings under cover can give golf-ball-
sized roots by late spring; later sowings provide summer, autumn and storage crops. Lift before roots become
woody or leathery. In mild winters some can stay outside, but severe frost can freeze and rot them.

Fresh carrots have a sweet, earthy flavour rarely matched by shop-bought roots. Establishment can be tricky:
germination is slow and slugs attack seedlings. Deep, fine, stone-free soil helps roots grow straight and tender;
light soil or compost-filled no-dig beds can work well. Sow short successions for early-summer to autumn
harvests. Steady moisture, careful thinning and carrot-fly protection make a big difference. Fresh manure,
compaction, stones, drought and rough handling can leave roots forked, tough or damaged.

Kohl rabi looks like a root crop, but the edible globe is really a swelling of the stem just above ground level. It is
a cabbage relative with a sweet, mild brassica flavour and a delicate texture when grown quickly and picked
young. The crop matures fast, often in about 8-12 weeks, making it useful as a half-season brassica. Quality
depends on steady moisture and timely harvesting: drought, checks to growth or leaving the globes too long can
make them hard and woody. Small young globes are good raw or cooked, while larger late varieties can stand
into autumn if they keep growing steadily.

A short entrance opens into a standing space,
with the bed wrapped around it. You can reach

Growing vegetables raises
plenty of questions: when to
sow, how much space to leave,
when to harvest, and what to do
when something starts eating
the crop before you do.
These illustrated guides bring
the useful details together, with
clear calendars, planting advice
and organic ways to tackle
common troubles.
There’s practical help with soil,
compost, watering and
greenhouses too. Use it to plan
your plot, then turn to the guide
you need as each job comes
along.
