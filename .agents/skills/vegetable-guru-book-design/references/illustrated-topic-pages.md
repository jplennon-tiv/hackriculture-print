# Illustrated topic-page composition

Read for a new illustrated gardening overview, practical sequence, comparison or
explanation. Apply with the parent book-design skill and the requested layout
review. This is a composition workflow, not an approved template or a second
design system. Colour, type, geometry and source approvals stay in the linked
[book design system](../../../../docs/BOOK-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md),
[asset registry](../../../../docs/assets/README.md) and current handover.

For A4, use the corresponding source designs and
[guide-layout-review](../../guide-layout-review/SKILL.md). The editorial questions
below remain useful, but book dimensions, gutters, type tiers and spread rules do
not transfer to A4. Do not force a general topic into a crop or Troubles schema.

## Establish the page's job

Start with the reader's intended outcome and a researched content brief. For new
gardening advice use [Gardening Research](/Users/johnlennon/.codex/skills/gardening-research/SKILL.md);
use supplied approved copy when the task is layout alone. Identify essential
actions, reasons, conditions and warnings, optional supporting material, and what
the illustrations must explain. Confirm the requested page/image count and units.

Select the actual output profile before composition: trim proof, supplier working
interior or rough screen preview. Read the current geometry rather than copying
numbers into this workflow. Inspect a relevant approved page's pixels and editable
source, particularly the illustrated how-to or another comparable interior.
Record which roles are inherited and which arrangements are new proposals.

An explicit one/two-page summary is a different brief from a complete crop guide
with permitted continuation pages. Curate the topic to its brief and retain
essential meaning. Do not silently add pages, reduce body type or remove a vital
condition. If necessary, show the concrete content/space choice that needs John's
decision after completing the work that can proceed independently.

## Compose before polishing the prose

Make a quick internal thumbnail/wireframe showing the title, lead, main reading
path, image slots, captions, supporting sections and footer clearance. Allocate
space from the chosen physical geometry and established type roles. Estimate
copy length from those slots, then test with actual copy; word counts alone do
not establish fit. Routine thumbnails need not become a separate approval round.

Choose a structure suited to the information, for example:

| Content | Possible composition, to adapt rather than treat as a fixed template |
| --- | --- |
| Practical sequence | Short orientation followed by clearly ordered steps, with drawings adjacent to the steps they explain |
| Comparison | Shared introduction and related columns/rows organised around the same reader questions, with space reflecting actual content |
| Mechanism or construction | Dominant explanatory diagram with short nearby sections/callouts; labels remain readable and followable |
| Overview with one hero | Expressive title/hero relationship, concise lead and a small number of clearly grouped advice sections |

Use a dominant visual or title relationship to establish entry, then a quieter
supporting hierarchy. A large heading, large hero and large coloured panel should
not all compete for first attention. Fit title line breaks deliberately, preserving
meaning, safe areas and space around the illustration. Do not force every topic
to use a crop opening's giant title, calendar, difficulty sticker or facts grid.

Align related headings, text edges, captions and image bounds to a small coherent
set of guides. Maintain useful line lengths and an unambiguous reading order.
Keep section headings with their opening text and captions with their pictures.
Avoid narrow text slivers around irregular art, isolated final words, or a caption
that visually attaches to the wrong image. Change image position or column shape
before distorting typography.

Use whitespace to separate ideas and allow the header and hero to breathe.
Judge optical balance: dark foliage or a coloured panel can outweigh a larger
pale area. Equal columns need not contain equal quantities of prose. Do not fill
every gap with a box, icon or invented advice; do not revive retired widget bars
or equal-height cards. Keep established section-specific rules when adapting an
existing vegetable/Troubles page; a new overview is a labelled new composition.

## Illustrations must have a job

For every image, record its purpose, subject, placement, approximate printed size,
decorative/instructional role, essential visible details, caption/labels and source
or generation plan. Respect the requested image count. Choose existing approved
art where it suits the role, checking its provenance and allocation.

Decorative heroes follow
[vegetable-guru-artwork](../../vegetable-guru-artwork/SKILL.md), including direct
approved style references, source comparison and asset records. A decorative hero
may support the topic without claiming to depict a diagnostic sign.

For instructional/diagnostic artwork, the evidence determines what must be shown.
Check sequence, quantities, layer relationships, scale cues, plant anatomy and
symptoms as relevant. Use credible subject references as well as house-style
references, and identify each input's role. Existing approved diagnostic/planting
drawings retain their useful detail; do not restyle them as decorative vegetables.

New explanatory art can share the house colour and drawing character only where
that preserves its meaning. Simplification must not invent a causal connection,
make uncertain symptoms look decisive, omit a necessary layer or obscure a useful
distinction. A generated image is a draft to verify, not horticultural evidence.
If a detail cannot be checked, revise or omit the misleading depiction and explain
the limit; aesthetic success does not compensate for a factual error.

For new raster art use the available image-generation skill/tool. Simple exact
schematics may use editable vector shapes, arrows and labels. Keep titles,
measurements, labels and captions in the editable layout/vector source, rather
than relying on generated lettering. Labels must identify the intended part,
leader lines must end clearly, and functional diagrams must stay complete inside
the safe area. Check meaning and legibility at printed size, not just zoomed in.

Retain separate assets and record subject/reference sources, prompts where used,
output paths/hashes, revisions and review status. Use the stock registry for assets
that belong to that set; otherwise retain a topic-specific artwork record beside
the working files and link it from the research record. New assets are proposals
until approved. Do not overwrite approved originals or imply all stock is approved.

## Two pages and facing spreads

Confirm physical folios before relying on a facing composition. Two consecutive
pages beginning on a recto are not a facing spread. When placement is unknown,
make independently intelligible pages and label any spread/parity assumption.
Do not change the book's blank-page policy merely to obtain a desired spread.

Where pages really face, balance their overall visual weight and connect related
headings or image rhythms. Avoid placing an essential sentence, label or diagram
across the binding. Give continuation headings useful context without repeating
the whole introduction. Functional symbols may repeat; large decorative headers
should not be mirrored copies unless the brief specifically calls for symmetry.

## Render, inspect and revise

Build a separate editable working layout using an appropriate existing pattern.
Read the applicable PDF and layout skills; inspect the actual affected PDFs after
fonts and images load. General topic pages need their own suitable working layout;
existing crop endpoints/checkers do not establish their fit automatically.

Review in three views:

1. At thumbnail size: entry point, hierarchy, overall balance, density and whether
   the one-page/two-page composition reads as intentional.
2. At intended reading size: title and body legibility, useful line lengths, caption
   relationships, sequence, contrast and informative illustration detail.
3. At close inspection: collisions, image edges, incorrect arrows/labels, widows,
   missing characters, font/image loading, binding safety and footer clearance.

Compare with the chosen approved source and relevant neighbouring page. Numerical
fit and a visually pleasing thumbnail are each incomplete checks. Revise the
largest compositional problem first: reading order and allocation, then image
scale/position and spacing, then small typographic details. Shorten copy through
the research and Write Like John workflows; repeat the factual check after cuts.

For each delivered proof, record the actual pages/units/spreads viewed, retained
exceptions and source comparison. Keep editable copy, layout and assets alongside
separate outputs. A review PDF is not supplier acceptance or John's approval.
After John approves a useful composition, register its exact source and status so
it can inform future pages; until then, describe it as a proposal. Do not build a
template gallery or regenerate the full collection as part of a single topic.
