VEGETABLE GROWING / Root Crops

Carrot & parsnip.

Troubles guide

Carrots and parsnips share several root pests and diseases. Carrots are especially vulnerable to carrot fly; parsnips also suffer from old seed and canker. Start with fresh seed, deep well-prepared soil and a clean seedbed. Avoid fresh manure; use resistant varieties where available.

Recognise→Act→Prevent

Parsnip

Old Seed / Poor Germination

recognise

Old seed, cold or dry soil and deep sowing can all delay or prevent emergence. Check conditions before resowing.

act

Sow again with fresh seed if the season still allows.

prevent

Use fresh seed each year. Keep the drill moist until seedlings emerge; a few radish seeds can mark the row.

Carrot

Green Top

recognise

Sunlight turns exposed carrot crowns green. Unlike green potatoes, green carrots are not harmful, but look unsightly.

prevent

Earth up around exposed crowns during the growing season.

Parsnip

Parsnip Rust Fungus

recognise

Rust-coloured crown damage may resemble staining or canker; the precise cause needs confirmation.

act

Lift and discard badly affected roots.

prevent

Improve drainage, avoid root injury and seek a diagnosis before choosing a long crop break.

Carrot

Clayburn

recognise

Carrot surface damage associated with clay; check for pests or rot. The cause is uncertain.

act

Inspect affected roots; seek identification for recurring damage. Discard active decay.

prevent

Prepare well-drained, workable soil; use a raised bed where necessary.

Carrot / Parsnip

Carrot Fly

Tunnelled roots

recognise

Leaves turn reddish, then yellow. Maggots tunnel in carrots or damage parsnip surfaces. Seedlings may die; roots may rot.

act

No cure. Minor surface damage on large parsnips may be trimmed away.

prevent

Cover completely with insect-proof mesh on rotated ground. Sow thinly. Carrots sown after mid-May or lifted before late August may avoid peak attacks.

Carrot / Parsnip

Fanging

Forked roots

recognise

Roots fork when tips meet stones, compaction or injury. Fresh manure and uneven seedbeds can contribute.

act

There is no treatment for an already forked root.

prevent

Prepare workable soil, remove larger stones and avoid compaction or fresh manure before sowing.

VEGETABLE GROWING / Root Crops

Carrot and Parsnip Troubles

Troubles guide

Recognise→Act→Prevent

Carrot

Small Roots

recognise

Crowding, drought, poor soil or pest damage can keep roots small. First check variety and maturity.

act

Check spacing, water and root health. Thin carefully and correct confirmed poor fertility.

prevent

Prepare workable soil in advance; maintain suitable spacing and steady moisture.

Carrot / Parsnip

Splitting

Split roots

recognise

Roots split after heavy rain or watering follows a dry spell. Leaving mature roots in wet autumn soil also increases the risk.

act

There is no cure. Use split roots immediately; they will not store.

prevent

Water regularly and deeply in drought. Mulch with compost to steady moisture. Where splitting recurs, lift maincrop carrots before prolonged wet weather.

Carrot / Parsnip

Sclerotinia Rot

Mouldy roots

recognise

Soft root or crown rot develops white cottony mould, sometimes with black resting bodies. Both growing crops and stored roots can be affected.

act

Remove infected plants and rotten stored roots promptly; keep them out of home compost.

prevent

Store sound roots and check regularly. Clear infected debris, improve drainage and avoid moving contaminated soil.

Carrot / Parsnip

Carrot-Willow Aphid

Distorted leaves

recognise

Greenfly cause distorted, discoloured, stunted leaves, especially in warm, dry summers. They can also transmit motley dwarf virus.

act

Tolerate small colonies; squash damaging colonies and encourage natural predators.

prevent

Inspect young crops and maintain steady growth. Aphid management cannot guarantee protection from viruses.

Carrot

Black Rot

Blackened carrot roots

recognise

Large black patches develop on stored carrots, often with no obvious symptoms before harvest.

act

Remove and destroy diseased roots promptly.

prevent

Store only sound roots in a cool, ventilated place and inspect regularly. Do not grow carrots on the same land next year. Avoid reusing a problem storage site year after year.

Carrot

Carrot Motley Dwarf

Discoloured leaves

recognise

Yellow mottling, red outer leaves and stunting may indicate carrot motley dwarf, an aphid-transmitted virus complex.

act

Confirm the cause and remove badly affected plants; infected plants cannot be cured.

prevent

Inspect young growth, encourage aphid predators and remove damaging colonies. Virus spread can still occur.
