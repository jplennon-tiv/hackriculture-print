Oriental leaves are quick salad and stir-fry crops, mostly brassicas: Chinese cabbage, leaf radish, mibuna,
mizuna, mustards, pak choi and shungiku. Many give baby leaves within a month. Late-summer sowings often
escape the worst flea beetle damage and fill the autumn salad gap; some leafy types crop into winter under
cover. These tender, flavoursome greens are for fresh use, not long storage.
Quick facts

Ready in From sowing: baby leaves ~3–4 weeks;

Recommended varieties
Mizuna
Quick Japanese leaf with finely cut
foliage and mild mustard flavour
for salads or brief cooking.
Mibuna
Narrow, strap-like Japanese green.
Use young leaves in salads, larger
ones in stir-fries.
Red Frills mustard
Finely divided, peppery red
mustard. Pick young and mix with
milder salad leaves.
Joi Choi
Crisp white-stalked pak choi with
green leaves. Pick young for
salads, larger for stir-fries.
Tatsoi
Low rosettes of dark leaves for
baby-leaf salads and quick
cooking.
Shungiku
Pungent chop suey greens. Pick
young shoots regularly before
flowering.
Key risks
Flea beetle
Flea beetles make small
round holes in young
brassica leaves, especially
during warm, dry weather.
Slugs
Slugs chew leaves and
seedlings, especially pak
choi in damp conditions.
Bolting
Cold checks, long days, heat
or drought can encourage
premature flowering,
depending on the species
and variety.
Cabbage
caterpillars
Chinese cabbage and other
brassica leaves may be
attacked by caterpillars.
