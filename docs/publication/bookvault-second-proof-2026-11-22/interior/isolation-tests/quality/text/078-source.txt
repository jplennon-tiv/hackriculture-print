Rhubarb is a long-lived perennial vegetable, usually cooked as fruit. Large crowns give tart spring stems, green
to red by variety; green stems are edible and colour does not show ripeness. Never eat the leaves. It suits cool
climates and winter dormancy. Moist, drained, compost-rich soil, feeding, mulching, spring watering and flower
removal keep crowns cropping for years. Yield varies with variety, age and conditions; leave enough foliage to
rebuild the crown.

Recommended varieties
Timperley Early
Early pink-and-green stems; also good for forcing to
start the season.
Victoria
Established main-season crop of substantial green-red
stalks for pies and preserves.
Raspberry Red
Tender, flavoursome red stalks. Buy named
crowns/plants for consistency.
Glaskin's Perpetual
Traditional, economical seed-raised rhubarb; expect
variation between plants.
Fulton's Strawberry Surprise
Vigorous plants with bright red stalks and a well￾balanced flavour. A useful maincrop choice.
Stockbridge Arrow
Long, thick red stalks with good flavour. Also suitable
for forcing.
Key risks
Crown rot
Decay at the crown can
cause weak, dull stems and
rotting buds.
Slugs under
forcing pots
Slugs may hide inside
forcing pots or buckets and
damage the tender forced
stems.
Poor growth or
stringy stems
Usually linked to dry soil,
lack of organic matter,
overcropping, or forcing the
same crown too often.
Honey fungus
Honey fungus can cause
rhubarb crowns to decline
and die.
