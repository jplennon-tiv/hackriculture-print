## Identifying the pest

The first entry is keyed as `troubles.Pests` in the [shared Mushroom record](../../../../../../hackriculture-data/vegetables/mushroom/mushroom.json). Its full advice explicitly names mushroom flies, its signs mention larvae and its existing fly illustration is appropriate. Both headings in the book proof now read **Mushroom flies**. The opening description distinguishes adults around the trays from larvae that damage the crop. The source supports the group name without identifying a single species. The Agri-Food and Biosciences Institute describes several fly families in mushroom cultivation and documents sciarid larvae feeding on mycelium and tunnelling into developing buttons. [AFBI mushroom pest guidance](https://www.afbini.gov.uk/article/insect-pests-mushrooms-sciaridae).

## Fancy making your own growing medium?

John’s undated *Straw Log Mushroom Kit Instructions* from Foragers Table Gourmet Mushrooms identify the crop as oyster mushrooms. The leaflet calls for bedding straw and boiling water, followed by cooling and draining before adding spawn in layers. The fruiting instructions call for air movement and light away from direct sun. The two original photographs are kept in the resource library. [Kit setup](../../../../../../hackriculture-resources/local/foragers-table-oyster-straw-log/instructions.jpg) · [Fruiting and tips](../../../../../../hackriculture-resources/local/foragers-table-oyster-straw-log/fruiting-and-tips.jpg).

An independent grower also describes pouring boiling water over straw, cooling it, then adding oyster spawn in layers. Containers, openings and incubation or fruiting conditions vary with the kit and strain. The printed example starts with bought oyster spawn and leaves quantities, soak times and openings to the supplier. John requested that the wording allow for any suitable wrapping or container the supplier recommends or supplies. [The Mushroom Conservatory’s growing guide, “Growing buckets of Oyster Mushrooms”, steps 2–4](https://themushroomconservatory.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/oyster-mushroom-growing-instructions.pdf).

The book opening describes kits generally, including oyster kits. The research on conditioned mushroom compost concerns cultivated button and chestnut mushrooms. The earlier panel about making that compost has been superseded by the oyster example. The withdrawn heap recipe in the master’s `metadata.deferred_key_notes` skipped conditioning and remains archival. The oyster leaflet concerns a different species and does not validate the withdrawn recipe. [Monaghan Mushrooms’ process for growing on compost](https://www.monaghan.eu/what-we-do/substrate/).

**Follow-up check, 6 November.** The comparison concerns preparing the growing medium from scratch with bought spawn. Florida's home oyster method uses heat-treated wheat straw. For home growers of brown `Agaricus bisporus`, UK spawn supplier MushroomBox describes partly composted stable bedding, pasteurisation and a later casing layer. A ready-spawned button kit supplies the prepared compost and casing. These are usual growing methods. Researchers have also cropped `A. bisporus` on lime-treated, pasteurised grass without conventional composting. That trial used a different process from the oyster leaflet. [University of Florida home oyster method](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/ss662) · [MushroomBox spawn instructions](https://mushroombox.co.uk/download/portabella_growing_instructions.pdf) · [Merryhill white button kit](https://www.merryhill-mushrooms.co.uk/merryhill-mushrooms-white-mushroom-growing-kit) · [Original trial of alternative growing media](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5483216/).

## Page 67 · casing and kit setup

Research check, 6 November, for the two sections headed **Soil & preparation** and **Starting a mushroom kit**. The comparison is confined to white button and cultivated brown chestnut mushrooms (*Agaricus bisporus*). Each of the three suppliers below instructs the home grower to spread a loose covering over the compost. Casing holds moisture and encourages mushrooms to form. [Penn State’s Agaricus growing guide](https://extension.psu.edu/basic-procedures-for-agaricus-mushroom-growing).

- Merryhill supplies a colonised block and separate casing for both its white and chestnut kits. Add the casing at setup and replace the loose lid. [Merryhill kit instructions](https://www.merryhill-mushrooms.co.uk/support).
- Suttons tells the grower to wait until white threads cover the compost, then spread the supplied top dressing loosely. The lid stays on except for spraying. [Suttons white button instructions](https://www.suttons.co.uk/all/mushroom-windowsill-kit-white-button_MH-12527).
- Mr Fothergill’s tells the grower to wait if the compost is still brown. Once white, spread the casing and leave the kit open. [Mr Fothergill’s white button instructions](https://www.mrfothergills.co.nz/blogs/all/white-button-mushroom-kits).

John accepted the revised page 67 copy with “Kit mushrooms” as its opening. In the context of the button and chestnut kit guide, Soil & preparation distinguishes the compost from the casing. Starting a mushroom kit describes the white threads, when to add the casing and how to keep it damp. Timing, temperature and lid position follow the instructions for the particular kit.
