I built my first food garden over 30 years ago. From childhood I had been fascinated by the way seeds became plants, the plants became vegetables, and the vegetables made a meal, all by natural processes. As a young adult gardener, I wanted to participate in this process. I felt that gardening could give me two things that I couldn't get in any other way: free or almost free food, and control over the chemicals in my diet.

It was natural to me from the beginning to grow organically. When I first began, organic gardening was at the fringes and I struggled to find the information I needed. A book of organic gardening techniques, as this one is, no longer has to be specially labelled or put in a special section; organic is mainstream gardening now, and the world is a better place for it.

Gardening books enter and leave our lives on a regular basis. Techniques and varieties change and need updating, and the books themselves (at least, the good ones!) end up being dropped onto the soil or left out in the rain and having to be replaced. Nothing will make me happier one day than to discover second-hand, battered copies of The Vegetable Guru with soil stains and notes in the margin: a book used the way I intended it, as an everyday guide rather than a coffee-table ornament.

The Vegetable Guru is a reference to be used. It is about facts, solid growing facts. You will find no artistic photographs or long backstories here; just solid growing information and the illustrations to support it, logically arranged on the pages, with the vegetable guides arranged alphabetically. The book has three main sections: common vegetables and their growing seasons and requirements; plant troubles and pests; and growing techniques.

I hope you enjoy using The Vegetable Guru as much as I enjoyed writing it. I wish you the best of success with your gardening, both now and in the future.

John Lennon — November 2026.
