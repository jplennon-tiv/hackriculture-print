Celery needs rich, moist, weed-free soil for crisp, well-flavoured stems. Traditional trench celery needs deep
preparation, regular watering and earthing-up to exclude light for winter stems. Self-blanching types are easier:
close planting and a dense canopy help blanch them, but they are less hardy and best cut before cold weather.
Celery leaf gives repeated pickings of smaller stems. Dry checks cause bitterness, stringiness and bolting; slugs
can spoil appearance without affecting flavour.

Recommended varieties
SELF BLANCHING
Victoria F1
Self-blanching celery with crisp, pale stalks. Easier
than trench types.
SELF BLANCHING
Golden Self-Blanching
Traditional pale self-blanching celery. Water regularly
for tender stalks.
SELF BLANCHING
Green Utah
Green-stemmed celery with a strong flavour. Keep
moist for steady growth and tender stalks.
TRENCH
Giant Red
Red-stemmed trench celery with a strong flavour.
Needs blanching and winter protection.
Key risks
Bolting
Bolting is a serious problem,
especially in dry seasons or
where young plants have
been checked.
Slugs and snails
Slugs and snails chew
celery leaves and stems,
especially in damp
conditions.
Celery fly (leaf
miner)
Larvae mine celery leaves,
making pale blotches that
turn brown and papery.
Celery leaf spot
(blight)
Brown or yellowish-brown
spots appear first on the
outer leaflets and then
spread to all of the foliage.
